Photographic Style Port

Adds the iPhone 16-generation Photographic Styles editing state to a HEIC from an older iPhone. Your photo never leaves this page.

Drop HEIC photos here on iPhone, choose Browse — not Photo Library
iPhone: pick through Files, not Photo Library. Choosing a photo from the Photo Library makes iOS convert it to JPEG before this page ever sees it, and a JPEG has none of the data the port needs. Get the original across once: in Photos tap Share → Save to Files, then come back here, tap the box above and choose Browse. Files picked that way arrive untouched.
Finished photos go straight to your library. Tap Save to Photos on a finished row, then pick Save Image in the share sheet — no renaming, no detour through Files.
Saved files arrive as .txt. This preview can only hand over a short list of file types, and HEIC is not one of them — the bytes are correct, so rename …_PhotographicStyle.txt to .heic and it opens normally.

Results

    What it needs

    A HEIC straight off an iPhone, with its embedded thumbnail and HDR gain map intact, in a supported tile layout (…). Photos that have been edited or re-exported by other software usually will not work — and on iPhone that includes anything picked from the Photo Library, which iOS converts to JPEG on the way in. Use Browse and pick from Files.

    What it keeps

    Your image data is copied across byte for byte — primary tiles, HDR gain map and thumbnail — so the visible photograph is untouched. Your Exif is preserved and only the single Apple MakerNote entry that gates the feature is added. Semantic mattes and the depth map come along too, so Portrait keeps working.

    Why Safari does better here

    Measuring your photo's own tone and light needs a HEIC decoder. Safari has one built in; other browsers do not, and this preview cannot load an external one. Without it the port still works, but falls back to neutral values instead of measurements — the row will say which one you got.

    Honest limits

    Reverse-engineering software, not an Apple-supported converter. It reproduces an editable Photographic Styles state; it does not reproduce Apple's capture-time rendering. Keep your originals.
